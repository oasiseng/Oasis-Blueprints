# The sheet standard

The aim is a set a homeowner, a framer and a plan reviewer can each follow without help, the way a
good assembly manual works: picture first, then steps, then the measured drawings.

## Who each sheet is for

**Cover (S0.0): the office and the plan reviewer.** The job in one sentence, a picture, how to read
the set, scope (new / existing / not in this set), design criteria, sheet index, job notes, and the
open items. A band at the bottom is reserved for the engineer's own general notes.

**Field sheet (S1.0): the person building it.** Headline and up to six key facts across the top.
Left: the picture with labels. Middle: plan above, section below. Right: the steps, each with a small
picture showing only what that step adds. Bottom: the connection details at large scale.

**Reserved sheets** hold a number for something that comes from elsewhere (a site plan, a survey).

## Colour is meaning

| Colour | Means | Never use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | New work under this permit | emphasis |
| Grey | Existing, stays | new work |
| Red + triangle | The engineer still owes this. Do not build from it | warnings in general |
| Orange | Hold point: stop and ask. Also temporary work (shoring) | new work |
| ± | Scanned, scaled or assumed. Verify in field | |

The same code is used on every view, in the picture, in the facts and in the notes, and the legend
states it on both sheets. A user's accent colour only tints their wordmark.

## Words

- Headline: one short sentence, plain words, the whole job. "New rafters, new columns, same sunroom."
- Say what to do, not what was considered. One action per step, in build order.
- Sentence case in panels and notes; UPPER CASE only inside drawings, where it is the drafting
  convention and stays legible at half size.
- Use the trade's ordinary abbreviations inside drawings only: (N), (E), O.C., TYP., V.I.F., SEE 4/S1.0.
- Name things the same way everywhere. If the plan says "column", the detail does not say "post".
- Numbers carry units. Dimensions that are not confirmed carry ±.
- Do not write an engineering requirement the user did not give. Write the open item instead.

## Scale and size

- Sheets are 24 × 36 in landscape. Print at 100% for the stated scale. An 11 × 17 print is
  reduced to fit, not exactly half size; verify legibility and do not use its original scale labels.
- Every measured view states its scale and carries a graphic scale bar; each sheet has a 1 in check
  square. The picture is labelled "Isometric · not to scale".
- Usual imperial scales: plan ½" or ¼" = 1'-0" (1:24, 1:48), section ¾" or ½" = 1'-0"
  (1:16, 1:24), details 3" or 1½" = 1'-0" (1:4, 1:8). Metric ratios such as 1:25 and 1:50
  are separate scales, not equivalents of those imperial labels. The plan is the largest view.

## Open items and status

- An open item is one question with one owner: what is needed, specifically, and who answers it.
- Each open item shows three ways: in the red list on the cover, as a red count in every title block,
  and in red at the place on the drawing where it applies.
- The status band reads "Draft · for engineer review · not for construction" until the status is
  changed, and the audit refuses `permit` or `construction` while any item is open.
- The title block keeps a blank box for the engineer of record's seal, signature and date. The kit
  never fills it.

## Before you deliver

1. Audit clean (no overflow, every view at its stated scale).
2. Previews looked at: no overlaps, every leader lands on what it names.
3. Every value on the sheets traces to something the user gave. Everything else is an open item.
4. The user's company is in the title block, and nobody else's.
